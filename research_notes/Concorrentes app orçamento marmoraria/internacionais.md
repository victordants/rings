# International countertop/stone fabrication software: quoting-to-approval-to-job benchmark (as of Nov 2026)

> Method note: in this session, direct page fetches (WebFetch) to moraware.com, slabos.com, stoneproject.app and capterra.com were **blocked by the sandbox network policy**, so every finding below comes from search-engine result summaries of those pages, not from reading full pages. Search summaries can lose or garble context. Treat exact numbers as "reported by the cited page, not read by me in full". Many sources are **vendor-authored competitor comparisons** (SlabWise, SlabOS, Stone Project, StoneMagnet, Stonify and QuoteIQ all publish "best X software" pages that rank themselves first). They are flagged where used. No Reddit r/countertops or Fabricator Forum threads surfaced in searches. That is a gap.

## Product profiles: value proposition, target size, platforms, pricing, trials

### Takeaway
The market splits into (a) legacy per-seat suites for mid-size shops (Moraware: about $100/user/month for quoting, plus $120/user/month with a 3-user minimum for scheduling), (b) new expensive flat-fee "shop OS" platforms (SlabOS: about $1,250–$1,500+/month), (c) mid-priced all-in-ones ($99–$350/month: ActionFlow Spring, SlabWise), and (d) mobile-first, small-shop tools (Stone Project, from about $79–$149/month, iOS/Android/web, with a Brazilian "Marmorarias" listing). For a one-person marmoraria, only Stone Project and generic field-service apps (Jobber, Housecall Pro, QuoteIQ) are priced and designed close to that segment.

## Quoting: drawing, pricing rules, options/alternatives

### Takeaway
The standard is a drawing tool that auto-computes square feet (area) and linear feet of finished edge, with sinks/cutouts/add-ons as priced items. Recent leaders add a "quick quote without drawing" mode, auto-nesting against real slabs and AI price-list import. Multi-material/option comparison in one quote is now claimed by Stone Project and the generic field-service apps, not clearly by Moraware.

### Cited Findings
- CounterGo: a 6-step drawing process (curves, bump-outs, custom splash, waterfalls, appliance edges, then sink/cooktop cutouts). It auto-calculates countertop sq ft for takeoff and linear ft of finished edge — [Moraware webinars](https://www.moraware.com/on-demand-webinars/)
- CounterGo fall 2024: square feet or measurements, plus curves, bump-outs, splash, edge, sinks and cutouts, "can all quickly be added without creating a Drawing" (a fast-quote mode) — [Moraware Fall 2024 recap](https://countergohelp.moraware.com/countergo-help/whats-new-moraware-recap-fall-2024)
- CounterGo also has a customer-facing website widget where visitors draw layouts and get estimates — [SlabWise comparison](https://slabwise.com/compare/countergo-vs-quickquote) (competitor-authored; unverified)
- SlabOS: quotes use real per-slab pricing, and "every quote nests against your real slabs automatically". Vendor claims "blank canvas to priced quote in under 3 minutes" (pitch deck) but also "SlabOS quote = 20 min avg across ~200 shops" (pricing page), which is internally inconsistent marketing — [SlabOS nesting](https://slabos.com/slab-nesting); [SlabOS pitch](https://slabos.com/pitch); [SlabOS pricing](https://slabos.com/pricing)
- SlabOS AI PDF price-list import: a vendor PDF price list becomes a priced catalog in about 90 seconds — [SlabOS pitch](https://slabos.com/pitch) (vendor claim)
- Stone Project: a quote builder with drawings, edges and cutouts. The client "approves the chosen stone option online", which implies multiple stone options per quote — [Stone Project estimating guide](https://www.stoneproject.app/best/countertop-estimating-software). Third-party limitation: no photo-calibrated vein matching on real slab images — [Stonify buying guide](https://www.stonify.io/resources/software-buying-guide/best-countertop-fabrication-software)
- Good/better/best: QuoteIQ on all plans (vendor claim). Housecall Pro add-on or Max tier. Jobber "Quote Options" in beta — sources as above

### Inferences
- The minimum viable pricing engine for a marmoraria: m² of stone by material (price per m²), linear meters of finished edge (acabamento de borda, per edge profile), backsplash/saia (rodabanca/saia) as m² or linear meters, per-unit cutouts (cuba, cooktop, torneira), per-unit add-ons (cuba itself, instalação, frete), plus a minimum charge. All of these map one-to-one to the CounterGo model.
- The highest-value UX pattern to copy: **one layout, multiple material options** (e.g. Granito São Gabriel vs Branco Itaúnas vs Quartzo). The total recalculates per material and the client picks online. Stone Project and SlabOS-style per-slab pricing point this way.
- "Quick quote without drawing" (fall 2024 CounterGo) suits on-site phone quoting: type dimensions by piece rather than drawing.

### Gaps
- No source detailed exact price-rule configuration (e.g. waste factor %, minimum per job) for any product.
- Could not verify whether CounterGo supports multiple materials in one quote.

## Customer-facing: online quote link, e-signature, deposit, portal, follow-ups

### Takeaway
Best in class (SlabWise, SlabOS, Stone Project, Jobber) is one branded link where the client views the quote, picks the option, e-signs and pays a deposit in a single step, with open/view tracking. Moraware is weakest here: PDF plus signature, no native payments or portal according to competitors.

### Cited Findings
- SlabWise: branded PDF plus portal link, real-time open/view/accept tracking, portal for approvals, signatures and deposits, Stripe built in — [SlabWise quick quote](https://slabwise.com/quick-quote) (vendor)
- SlabOS: branded, **login-free** customer portal covering approvals, files and payments, with "deposit + final split" — [SlabOS pricing](https://slabos.com/pricing) (vendor)
- Stone Project: branded PDF with a client link to approve and pay a deposit. White-label client portal on Pro — [estimating guide](https://www.stoneproject.app/best/countertop-estimating-software); [Terms](https://www.stoneproject.app/terms)
- Moraware/CounterGo: customer-facing PDF quote with signature support. Competitors say there is no client portal, no native payment collection and that "native notification tools require add-ons or third-party integrations" — [SlabWise CounterGo review](https://slabwise.com/reviews/countergo-review); [SlabWise vs CounterGo](https://slabwise.com/compare/slabwise-vs-countergo); [Fervor Studio](https://fervorstudio.ca/news/moraware-review-pricing-alternatives/) (all competitor or third-party, unverified)
- Jobber: Client Hub approval, drawn or typed signature, "Approve and Pay Deposit" — [Quotrr](https://quotrr.com/guides/best-quoting-software-for-contractors)
- Housecall Pro: e-sign records signer details, IP and timestamp — [Clean Savannah](https://www.cleansavannah.com/post/best-proposal-software-for-deck-builders-2026)
- ActionFlow: legally binding e-signature via SignNow — [ActionFlow](https://www.actionflow.net/solutions/process-development-automation/esignature/)

### Inferences
- For Brazil: replace Stripe/ACH with **PIX for the sinal (deposit)**, and email/SMS with a **WhatsApp link**. A login-free link (SlabOS approach) is essential, since Brazilian clients will not create accounts.
- Record IP, timestamp, name and CPF on acceptance (Housecall Pro approach) for a simple "aceite eletrônico" with evidentiary value under Brazilian law (MP 2.200-2/2001 recognizes non-ICP-Brasil electronic signatures when the parties accept them. This is legal background, not from the sources).
- "Quote viewed" tracking plus an automatic follow-up reminder is cheap to build and a known selling point (SlabWise).

### Gaps
- Found no specifics on automated follow-up cadences (e.g. reminder after X days) for any stone-specific product. SMS/WhatsApp support is unconfirmed for all products.

## Job flow after approval: scheduling, templating/install, work orders, inventory, status

### Takeaway
After approval the canonical pipeline is: quote → job → template (medição) appointment → digital job packet/work order → fabrication (with slab/remnant assignment and nesting) → install → invoice/final payment. Moraware splits this into separate paid modules. Newer tools bundle it.

### Cited Findings
- Moraware: convert a CounterGo quote to a Systemize job for scheduling, digital job files and progress tracking from template through install. Inventory is a separate $50/user module — [Moraware FAQ](https://www.moraware.com/faq/); [Moraware pricing](https://www.moraware.com/pricing/)
- Easy Stone Shop: per-piece printed labels from the CAD drawing for shop-floor tracking — [SourceForge](https://www.sourceforge.net/software/product/Easy-Stone-Shop)
- Stone Project: inventory with LiDAR/camera measurement of a piece (remnant) written onto the inventory record. The vendor says this is "a catalog measurement, not a cut list" — vendor pages via search summary [stoneproject.app](https://www.stoneproject.app/); [User Guide](https://help.stoneproject.app/). Its Remnant Finder marketplace lets shops buy and sell leftovers — same
- SlabOS: automatic slab nesting, QuickBooks sync, an AI assistant answering plain-language questions about jobs, quotes, invoices and pipeline — [SlabOS nesting](https://slabos.com/slab-nesting); [SFA release](https://www.openpr.com/news/4633871/slabos-announces-sfa-guardian-sponsorship-as-countertop-shops)
- ActionFlow Spring: drawing plus scheduling bundled — [Stone Project review](https://www.stoneproject.app/best/countertop-software)

### Inferences
- A one-person shop needs a much lighter version: a status board (Orçamento enviado → Aprovado/Sinal pago → Medição agendada → Em produção → Instalação agendada → Concluído/Pago) plus a calendar of measurements and installs plus a simple remnant (retalho) list with photo and size. Nesting and multi-station shop-floor tracking are overkill for one person.
- A remnant inventory linked to quoting (offer a cheaper quote from existing retalhos) is a cheap, valuable feature borrowed from Stone Project.

### Gaps
- No detail found on automated customer notifications at status changes (e.g. "installation scheduled") for any product.

## Strengths, weaknesses, what to copy, what to avoid

### Takeaway
Copy the newcomers' single "view, choose, sign, pay" link, the CounterGo pricing model (area + edge linear + cutouts + add-ons) with a no-drawing quick mode, and per-option material comparison. Avoid per-seat, module-fragmented, opaque or call-for-price pricing, desktop-only workflows and heavy ERP scope.

### Cited Findings
- Strengths: Moraware has a proven pricing model and quote→job integration ([Moraware FAQ](https://www.moraware.com/faq/)). SlabOS/SlabWise offer an integrated portal with deposit and tracking ([SlabOS pricing](https://slabos.com/pricing); [SlabWise](https://slabwise.com/quick-quote)). Stone Project is mobile-first, has PIX and a Brazilian listing ([App Store PT-BR](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/stone-project-marmorarias/id1443840165?l=pt-BR)).
- Weaknesses: Moraware has a dated UI, a training burden and module costs ([SlabWise review](https://slabwise.com/guide/moraware-review-2026)). SlabOS needs a sales call and costs $1,250–$1,500+/month plus a $3,000 setup fee ([SlabOS pricing 2026](https://slabos.com/countertop-software-pricing-2026)). Stone Project has inconsistent pricing and trial terms and few reviews ([Terms](https://www.stoneproject.app/terms); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/stone-project-countertops/id1443840165)). QuoteIQ sends static quotes with no live option toggling ([Service Business Academy](https://servicebusinessacademy.org/quoteiq-crm-review-2026/)).

### Inferences
**Copy:**
1. A quote builder by piece (bancada, ilha, lavatório, soleira, rodapé) with auto m², linear m of edge, cutouts and add-ons, plus a "rapid mode" with no drawing.
2. Multiple material options per quote, with the client choosing on the link (interactive, unlike QuoteIQ's static quote).
3. A login-free branded link sent by WhatsApp: view → choose option → accept (name/CPF, IP, timestamp) → pay the sinal via PIX → status updates.
4. "Viewed" tracking plus an automatic follow-up reminder.
5. One-tap conversion of an approved quote to an order (pedido) with a status board and a measurement/install calendar.
6. A remnant (retalho) list with photo and size, usable in quotes.
7. AI import of supplier price lists and AI sketch-to-pieces as differentiators.

**Avoid:**
- Per-user, multi-module pricing. Hidden or "call us" pricing. Inconsistent prices across pages.
- Forcing a CAD-style drawing for every quote (Moraware's training complaint).
- Requiring clients to log in or create an account.
- Overbuilding ERP features (nesting, multi-location, shop-floor stations) for a solo shop.
- Treating LiDAR measurements as final.
